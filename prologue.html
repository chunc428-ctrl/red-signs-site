Prologue

Red has always been my favorite color, so I naturally started photographing red things I noticed around New York. At first, I was simply collecting a color I liked. But after looking through the photographs, I realized that many of the red objects I had photographed were signs: NO PARKING signs, PRIVATE PROPERTY notices, NO SMOKING warnings, MTA signs, fire-safety labels, sprinkler and standpipe signs, warnings on trucks, and other instructions attached to buildings and streets.

What interested me was that these signs did not belong to one system. Some came from city agencies, some from buildings, businesses, vehicles, or private property owners. They also did not all use one exact standardized red. Still, when I placed the photographs next to one another, the colors made the signs feel related. Again and again, this kind of red appeared when a message wanted to be noticed, taken seriously, or separated from everything around it.

This book follows that red through New York City. Rather than documenting one official signage system, it brings together unrelated signs that share a similar visual language and asks why red appears so often around instructions, restrictions, warnings, and important information.
